## Claim
Interleaving written reasoning with tool actions and their observations makes an LLM agent more grounded, easier to audit and better at interactive tasks than reasoning-only or acting-only prompting.
## Mechanism
- The model emits a thought, then an action, then receives an observation, repeating until done.
- Thoughts plan, track progress, and handle exceptions; actions pull in facts from outside the model.
- Only a few hand-written trajectories (one or two for interactive tasks) are used as in-context examples.
- The full trajectory stays readable, so a human can inspect why each step happened.
- Combining ReAct with chain-of-thought self-consistency gave the best HotpotQA and Fever results.
## Evidence
- ALFWorld: best ReAct trial reached 71% success versus 45% for the best Act-only trial and 37% for BUTLER.
- Abstract reports absolute gains of 34% (ALFWorld) and 10% (WebShop) over imitation and RL methods.
- Fever: ReAct 60.9 versus CoT 56.3; HotpotQA: ReAct 27.4 slightly behind CoT 29.4.
- Error analysis: CoT hallucination was 56% of its failures; ReAct had 0% in that category.
## Limits
- The rigid thought/action/observation format lowers reasoning flexibility; reasoning errors were 47% of ReAct failures.
- Empty or unhelpful search results (23% of failures) derail the trajectory.
- Prompting a small model with ReAct performed worst on HotpotQA until it was fine-tuned.
## We adopt
Every substantial step is logged as thought, action, observation in LEDGER.md (DESIGN.md#react-steps), and the PreCompact hook preserves that ledger across context compaction (DESIGN.md#compaction-ledger).
## We reject
We do not rely on free-form inline reasoning as the record. Traces must be written to files, because context is lost on compaction. We also do not copy the Wikipedia-search setup or fixed few-shot exemplars.
