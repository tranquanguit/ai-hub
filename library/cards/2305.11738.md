## Claim
Models correct their outputs reliably when the critique step is grounded in external tools, and unreliably when they only review themselves.
## Mechanism
- Produce an initial answer.
- Choose a tool for each aspect to check (search engine, code interpreter, toxicity classifier).
- Run the tool and treat its output as the critique.
- Revise the answer from that feedback; iterate a few rounds.
## Evidence
- ChatGPT: +7.7 F1 across three QA tasks (AmbigNQ, TriviaQA, HotpotQA).
- ChatGPT: +7.0% absolute across three mathematical reasoning tasks.
- ChatGPT: 79.2% lower toxicity probability on the toxicity task.
- Authors report that LLMs are unreliable at validating their own results; self-only correction gave modest or negative change.
## Limits
- Gains depend on a tool existing for the property being checked.
- Benefits shrink after the first few rounds.
- Tested on QA, programs and toxicity, not long-running agent workflows.
## We adopt
Verification steps call scripts and graders (tools/promote.py, evals) instead of asking the model whether it is right; this is RULES.md#r2, enforced at DESIGN.md#external-feedback-gate.
## We reject
Search-engine fact-checking inside routine tasks; verified facts come from the owner or source documents, not live web lookups.
