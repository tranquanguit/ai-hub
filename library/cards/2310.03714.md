## Claim
LM pipelines should be declared as modules and then optimised automatically against an explicit metric, instead of being hand-tuned as prompt strings.
## Mechanism
- Signatures declare what each step does; modules wrap prompting techniques.
- A compiler (teleprompter) takes the program, a small training set and a metric.
- It simulates the program, keeps demonstrations that lead to metric-passing outputs, and bootstraps missing labels.
- Compiled variants are compared on a dev set; optimisers can be composed with a teacher program.
- Metrics may themselves be programs.
## Evidence
- GSM8K with GPT-3.5: vanilla 25.2% test; bootstrapped chain-of-thought program 72.9%; human-written CoT few-shot 72.4%.
- Abstract: gains generally over 25% (GPT-3.5) and 65% (llama2-13b-chat) versus plain few-shot prompting.
- Compiled small models were competitive with expert-written prompt chains on GPT-3.5 for HotpotQA.
## Limits
- Only two case studies (GSM8K, HotpotQA); broader tasks left to future work.
- Result quality depends on the metric and a representative training set.
- Dev-set gains exceed test gains in several rows, which signals overfitting risk.
## We adopt
The improve skill optimises a prompt or skill against a stated metric and keeps the change only if the metric rises, otherwise reverts (.claude/skills/improve, RULES.md#r6, DESIGN.md#improve-loop).
## We reject
The DSPy framework and its programming model, and fine-tuning. We tune plain text files and need no compiler; held-out checks guard against overfitting.
