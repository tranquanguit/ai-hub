## Claim
Agent memory can be analyzed systematically by its sources, forms and operations, and by how it is evaluated.
## Mechanism
- Sources: information inside a trial, across trials, and external knowledge.
- Forms: textual versus parametric memory, each with trade-offs.
- Operations: writing (projection), management (summarize, merge, forget) and reading (retrieval).
- Evaluation is direct (subjective and objective measures) or indirect (task success such as dialogue or QA).
- A unified formulation shows how earlier agents differ in these functions.
## Evidence
- Survey: no new experiments or benchmark numbers.
- Tables classify prior systems by memory source and memory form.
- Identifies parametric memory and multi-agent memory as future directions.
## Limits
- A taxonomy, not evidence on which design works best.
- Coverage ends in early 2024 and misses later agentic-memory work.
- Little guidance on write-time validation or conflict handling.
## We adopt
Its source/form/operation breakdown as an audit checklist for our memory design (DESIGN.md#memory-tiers): for each tier we confirm where content comes from, its form, and who may write, manage and read it.
## We reject
Parametric memory (fine-tuning or editing weights): we use a hosted model and want every memory inspectable and revertible as text. We also do not adopt automatic forgetting without review.
