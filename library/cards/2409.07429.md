## Claim
Agents do better on long-horizon tasks when they induce reusable sub-task workflows from past successful trajectories and supply the relevant ones to later runs.
## Mechanism
- An induction module reads experiences (instruction plus action trajectory) and extracts workflows.
- Workflows are deliberately fine-grained sub-routines, such as searching for a product, not whole tasks.
- Offline mode induces from training examples; online mode induces from successful test runs and keeps growing the memory.
- Workflows are added to agent memory as extra context for subsequent tasks.
## Evidence
- WebArena: 12.0 absolute points and 51.1% relative gain in overall success over BrowserGym.
- Mind2Web: 24.6% relative success-rate gain; also fewer steps per successful WebArena task.
- Online AWM beats baselines by 8.9 to 14.0 absolute points as train-test gaps widen.
- Describing states in natural language beat raw HTML by 0.8 step-success points.
## Limits
- Web navigation only; no claim for other domains.
- Widening the action space with workflows added little: workflows were called in just 18.5% of tasks.
- Adding both NL state descriptions and filtered HTML lengthened context and scored worse than either alone.
## We adopt
skill-forge induces a workflow from repeated successful tasks and drafts a skill after two successes, then verification gates promotion (.claude/skills/skill-forge, DESIGN.md#skill-library).
## We reject
Online induction straight into memory without a check. Our drafts stay unpromoted until a test, tool or human confirms them.
